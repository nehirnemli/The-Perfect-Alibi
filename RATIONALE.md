# Design Rationale — THE PERFECT ALIBI

## 1. Idea

THE PERFECT ALIBI is an interactive detective experience about the disappearance of a valuable painting during a private gallery exhibition in İzmir. Participants explore suspects, examine evidence, follow a timeline, and make a final verdict. Rather than presenting the mystery as a story to read, the project turns investigation into an active experience. Its design aims to balance participant choice with a controlled process of discovery.

## 2. Interactive Premises: Agency and Possibility

The project combines Agency and Possibility. Agency is expressed through the participant's ability to explore suspect profiles, examine evidence, interpret information, and submit a verdict. The interface responds through reviewed labels, timeline updates, and a final reveal. However, this agency is limited: participants can make an investigative decision, but cannot change the underlying story or its outcome.

Possibility is expressed through sequential evidence unlocking. Each evidence file becomes available only after the previous one has been reviewed. This rule structures the investigation and determines which actions are possible at each stage. It encourages gradual discovery, but also restricts the freedom to inspect evidence in any order.

## 3. Theoretical Framework

Wardrip-Fruin et al. (2009) describe agency as a relationship between the actions players want to take and those supported by a system. This informed the decision to let participants investigate suspects and submit their own verdict, with visible interface responses. However, the fixed narrative limits the consequences of their choices.

Gaver (1991) introduces sequential affordances, where one action reveals information that makes another action possible. This concept informed the evidence-unlocking rule: reviewing one file enables access to the next. The sequence guides attention and supports gradual discovery, although it limits freedom of navigation.

## 4. What I Learned

A participant who had not previously seen the project completed the experience independently without getting stuck. They noticed that evidence files unlocked sequentially and praised the screen design and story structure, describing the ending as unexpected. Based on this test, I decided to keep the sequential unlocking rule and avoid adding unnecessary instructions, since the participant understood the investigation without help.
